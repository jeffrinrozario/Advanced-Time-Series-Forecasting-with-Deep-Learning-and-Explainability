Comparative Analysis Findings

Based on the performance metrics (MAE, MSE, RMSE) and the visual comparison of actual vs. predicted values, we can draw the following conclusions:

1. Overall Performance:
   - LSTM Model: The deep learning LSTM model significantly outperformed all traditional baseline models (ARIMA, Exponential Smoothing, Prophet) across all evaluated metrics (MAE, MSE, RMSE).
     - LSTM MAE: 3.39
     - LSTM MSE: 25.96
     - LSTM RMSE: 5.09
   - Traditional Models: ARIMA, Exponential Smoothing, and Prophet models showed considerably higher error scores, indicating their limited ability to capture the complex patterns in this multi-variate time series data.
     - ARIMA MAE: 64.63, MSE: 6453.90, RMSE: 80.34
     - Exponential Smoothing MAE: 53.09, MSE: 4540.12, RMSE: 67.38
     - Prophet MAE: 50.50, MSE: 3386.65, RMSE: 58.19

2. Model Stability:
   - LSTM: The LSTM model's predictions appear visually more stable and closely follow the actual price movements, showing less volatility and fewer large deviations compared to the baseline models. This suggests better generalization and robustness to the unseen test data.
   - Traditional Models:
     - ARIMA: The ARIMA model appears to produce a relatively flat forecast, often struggling to capture turning points or significant changes in trend. This is likely due to the inherent limitations of linear models in highly dynamic, non-linear financial time series, even after differencing.
     - Exponential Smoothing: This model provides a somewhat smoother forecast than ARIMA but also struggles with accurately predicting sharp changes. Its stability is moderate, generally lagging behind actual movements.
     - Prophet: While showing better performance than ARIMA and ETS, Prophet's predictions still exhibit noticeable deviations from the actual values, particularly when the market shows higher volatility. It tends to capture overall trends but misses finer movements.

3. Reasons for Performance Differences:
   - LSTM's Superiority: The LSTM's ability to learn long-term dependencies and complex non-linear relationships within sequential data is a key factor in its superior performance. Financial time series often exhibit such intricate patterns that linear models like ARIMA or simpler exponential smoothing cannot effectively model. The multi-variate nature of the input (AAPL, MSFT, GOOG) also benefits from LSTM's capacity to process multiple correlated series simultaneously.
   - Traditional Model Limitations: These models often rely on assumptions of stationarity, linearity, or specific seasonality patterns. While differencing helped with stationarity, the underlying complexity and interdependencies of stock prices are not fully captured by these simpler approaches. Their single-series focus also means they don't explicitly leverage the correlation between different stocks as effectively as a multi-variate deep learning model might.

4. Interpretability (SHAP insights):
   - While the quantitative metrics clearly favor LSTM, traditional models offer higher inherent interpretability due to their simpler mathematical foundations. However, the SHAP analysis for the LSTM model provided insights into feature importance. For example, the waterfall plot (not fully detailed here for brevity, but observed in the previous step) showed that recent past values of the predicted ticker itself, as well as recent values of correlated tickers, were significant drivers of the predictions. This helps bridge the gap between complex deep learning models and the need for understanding their decisions, providing a level of post-hoc interpretability that confirms intuitive dependencies.

In summary, the deep learning LSTM model significantly outperforms the traditional baselines in forecasting multi-variate stock prices, demonstrating better accuracy and stability due to its capacity to learn complex temporal and inter-series relationships. While traditional models are more interpretable by design, frameworks like SHAP can provide valuable insights into the deep learning model's decision-making process.
